OUR STORY

Nothing Lasts Forever.

People say memories are eternal, but Alzheimer’s pauses eternity.

At the very beginning, our team consisted of just two people. We did not have a clear idea yet but we simply wanted to create an immersive interactive website that could offer a different way of experiencing stories. As the concept evolved, we realised that illustrations would make the experience far more engaging, so Emily joined the team as our illustrator. Through further discussions, we also discovered that immersion relies heavily on sound, ambience, and music, which led Fatih to become our sound designer. By the end of our journey, our team had grown into a collaboration between a web developer, a storyteller, an illustrator, and a sound designer. This combination allowed us to move beyond traditional storytelling formats such as books, videos, and films, and explore a new way of telling stories through an immersive web experience.

When we first began the project, we did not have a specific theme in mind. As we explored different ideas together, our discussions naturally led us to Alzheimer’s disease. One of our team members, Elin, had previously created a short film about Alzheimer’s during her undergraduate studies. This project became an opportunity to revisit the subject from a completely different perspective.

Rather than asking audiences to observe Alzheimer’s from the third perspective, we wanted them to experience fragments of the condition from first view. Through interaction and storytelling, we hoped to move beyond traditional narrative forms and create a more personal and emotional understanding of memory loss.

To Elin, Alzheimer’s is different from illnesses such as cancer. It is not simply a disease that brings people closer to death. Instead, it feels like a long tug-of-war between a person and their own memories, gradually taking away independence and the sense of self. That perspective became the emotional foundation of our project and ultimately shaped Alzheimer’s into the central theme of our storytelling.

As populations continue to age, dementia is becoming an increasingly significant global health challenge. According to the World Health Organization (WHO), around 57 million people worldwide were living with dementia in 2021, with nearly 10 million new cases diagnosed every year. Alzheimer’s disease is the most common form of dementia, accounting for approximately 60–70% of all dementia cases. Rather than asking people to learn about it through articles or medical descriptions alone, we wanted to create an experience that allows visitors to feel, even briefly, what living with memory loss might be like.

Besides, Somewhere Still is an immersive narrative website built around three interconnected stories: Margaret, Megan, and Thomas. Together, they represent three of the relationships that shape our lives · family, friendship, and love. In many ways, these relationships are also what we spend our lives searching for and holding onto.

Memories may fade,
the love and connections we share remain somewhere, still.

Still here. Somewhere still.
